Framework · Queensland local government areas

AI Disaster Resilience Index

Take a validated Australian index built for bushfires and floods, keep its architecture, and swap the hazard. Six catastrophic AI outcomes, one scoreable exposure factor each, every factor computable from public LGA-level data.

The move

Borrow the architecture, replace the hazard

The Australian Disaster Resilience Index scores every Australian LGA on coping capacity and adaptive capacity across eight themes and 82 indicators — social character, economic capital, infrastructure and planning, emergency services, community capital, information access, governance and leadership, and social and community engagement. It is peer-reviewed, government-published, and already at the geography you need.

Its structure is hazard-agnostic by design. It does not measure “how much bushfire is coming”; it measures whether a community can absorb a shock and adapt afterwards. That is exactly the part that transfers. What does not transfer is the hazard model — so that is the part you have to build, and the part you must be honest about.

The one sentence that makes this defensible. The hazards are hypothetical; the vulnerability is measured. A community that cannot reach an intensive care bed cannot reach one regardless of what caused the emergency. A council that is the sole water provider for an isolated town is the sole water provider whether the outage comes from a flood or an intrusion. You are not predicting AI catastrophes — you are mapping who absorbs a shock badly, using indicators already validated for shocks that do happen.

What people are actually worried about

The hazard taxonomy, from the authoritative source

Don't invent a risk list. Use the International AI Safety Report 2026 — published 3 February 2026, led by Yoshua Bengio, over 100 experts, expert panel nominated by more than 30 countries including Australia. It sorts risks into three categories.

Malicious use

Someone intentionally uses AI to cause harm. Cyberattacks, deepfakes, and biological weapons uplift. The report notes AI agents identifying 77% of software vulnerabilities in competition settings.

Malfunctions

Systems fail or operate outside intended parameters. Hallucinations, evaluation gaming, and loss-of-control behaviours including sandbagging and reward hacking.

Systemic risks

Broad societal harms from widespread deployment. Labour market disruption and threats to human autonomy.

Citing this rather than a list of your own does three things: it is unimpeachable as a source, it is internationally legible, and it saves you from being told you picked the risks that suited your data.

Provenance

Where the six came from

An honest answer, because it is the first thing you will be asked.

The three-category taxonomy is sourced — International AI Safety Report 2026. The six specific outcomes are not. They are a derivation: the taxonomy mapped onto the things Australian local government actually touches. Nobody has published a local-government AI hazard register, so there was nothing to cite. Say that out loud rather than implying the list is authoritative.

What makes the derivation defensible is a stated selection rule. A hazard earns a place only if all four hold:

TestWhy
1 · Traces to the taxonomyIt is a recognised category of AI risk, not one you invented to fit your data.
2 · Lands through a local channelLocal government either controls the channel, or the community's exposure through it differs by place.
3 · Varies measurably by LGAThere is public data that separates one council from another. A uniform national risk belongs in someone else's report.
4 · Somebody local can actA risk a council can do nothing about produces a scary map and no decision.
Why water and not electricity — and why that answer was too quick. Water and sewerage passed test 2 easily: in regional Queensland the council is the utility. Electricity failed it, because distribution and generation are state-owned and no council runs a grid. But that reasoning conflated who operates the asset with how exposed the community is, and the index measures the second. Queensland has communities served by isolated electricity networks rather than the national grid, and an isolated network is a materially different risk object from a grid connection. So H1 is now essential services, with water/sewerage provision and isolated-network dependence as separate inputs. The same test should be re-run on telecommunications.

Correction

Remoteness is not a curse

The single biggest error in the first draft, and fixing it is where this framework earns its keep.

Treating remoteness as monotonic vulnerability is wrong, and obviously wrong once stated. A town of 400 can seal its entry and exit; a city of two million cannot. That is not hypothetical — during 2020, Commonwealth biosecurity determinations restricted entry to designated remote communities, and those communities were markedly protected as a result. Forty staff who know every ratepayer by name can run the rates on paper for a fortnight. Brisbane City Council cannot. A fabricated message from "the council" is checkable in a small town by walking down the street; in a capital city there is no social path to verification at all.

The error was collapsing three different things into one variable. Separate them and remoteness stops having a single sign:

HazardExposure — does it reach youAbsorptive — can you contain it locallyConsequence — how bad if it landsNet
H1 essential servicesNeutralWorse — one plant, one operatorWorse — days to restoreRemote worse
H2 biologicalBetter — can seal, low throughputBetter — cohesion, known populationFar worse — no ICUDepends on which term dominates
H3 synthetic warningNeutralBetter — short verification chain, everyone knows the mayorWorse — fewer channels, thinner coverageGenuinely ambiguous
H4 service substitutabilityNeutralWorse — no alternative providerWorse — nowhere else to goRemote worse
H5 institutionalBetter — smaller attack surface, fewer systemsBetter — manual fallback is realWorse — no IT capability to respondPlausibly remote better
H6 labourWorse — single-industry concentrationWorse — thin labour marketWorse — no alternative employerRemote worse

Two of six flip or become ambiguous, and H5 plausibly inverts entirely — a highly digitised metropolitan council with two hundred integrated systems and no manual process is more exposed to institutional failure than a shire that still runs on paper. That inversion is the most interesting thing in the whole framework, and the first draft would have hidden it.

Build every factor with all three terms explicit. Exposure × (1 − absorptive capacity) × consequence, with the sign on remoteness set per term rather than per hazard. If you cannot say which of the three a variable belongs to, you do not understand what you are measuring yet.

The validation that matters

How to know you have not just rebuilt ADRI

You asked the right question. Here is the test.

If you take the same inputs and make the same assumptions, you will reproduce the Australian Disaster Resilience Index with extra steps, and a judge will say you rediscovered that remote communities are disadvantaged. The defence is not an argument — it is a number.

ADRI is downloadable at LGA level. So compute the rank correlation between your index and theirs, and report it as a headline result.

  • If the correlation is very high — say above 0.9 — you have added nothing, and the honest finding is worth publishing anyway: AI risk exposure tracks existing disaster vulnerability closely enough that councils should reuse the resilience work they have already done. That is a useful, decision-relevant null result, and reporting it will impress a research panel more than a contrived divergence.
  • If the correlation is moderate, the divergence is the finding. Name the councils that move most and explain which factor moved them.

There are real reasons to expect divergence, and they are all things natural-hazard indices have no reason to contain:

Novel variableNatural-hazard analogueExpected effect
Vendor concentrationNone at allTwo councils with identical ADRI scores can have completely different exposure. This is the cleanest source of divergence available to you.
System dependence / digitisationInverted — infrastructure is a strength in ADRIPushes well-resourced metro councils up the risk ranking. The signature result if your index is doing anything.
Digital inclusionMarginalCentral here, peripheral there.
Population densityProtective for natural hazardsInverts for contagion and for sealability.
Pre-register this prediction before you run it. If our index is measuring something new, highly digitised metropolitan councils will rank worse on H3 and H5 than they do on ADRI, and the overall rank correlation will fall below 0.9. Write it down, timestamp it, then test it. If the prediction fails, you report that — and you will still have the only LGA-level AI exposure map in the country, plus a clean demonstration that it does not tell you anything ADRI did not.

The register

Six outcomes, six scored factors

Each one: the cataclysm in plain words, a single exposure factor scoreable per LGA, the formula, and where the data lives. Read these after the remoteness correction above — the formulas below still need their three terms separated out.

H1Malicious use · cyber weight high

Intrusion into essential services

Water, sewerage or an isolated electricity network is compromised or interrupted in a community with no alternative supply and no rapid restoration path.

Scored factor — unregulated essential-service dependency

Under the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018, a critical water asset is one serving at least 100,000 connections, and only then does the responsible entity owe a critical infrastructure risk management program. Almost every Queensland council that operates water and sewerage falls below that line.

So the finding is built into the factor: these councils run critical infrastructure that is not regulated as critical infrastructure, and the smaller and more remote the council, the truer that is.

Electricity belongs here too, but as exposure rather than operation. No Queensland council runs a grid — yet a community on an isolated network rather than the interconnected grid is a different risk object, and that difference is a property of the place, not of who holds the licence.

H1 = max( is_water_provider × (1.5 if connections < 100,000), isolated_electricity_network × 1.2 ) × (1 / restoration_access_score) # consequence: days to restore × (1 / local_operator_depth) # absorptive: one operator or a team
Data
  • Water and sewer connections per council — QLD comparative information report
  • Remoteness class — ABS ASGS
  • SOCI threshold — CISC water and sewerage guidance
Why it varies by LGA

South-east councils have separated water into distributor entities. Regional and remote councils are the utility, often with one plant, one operator, and days of road access.

H2Malicious use · biological weight high

Biological event reaching an isolated community

A pathogen arrives somewhere that cannot isolate, cannot treat, and cannot evacuate at speed.

Scored factor — time to critical care under isolation

This is the single most defensible factor in the register, because every input is a validated vulnerability measure already used for natural hazards and pandemic planning. Nothing about it depends on believing a particular AI story.

Household overcrowding is the transmission multiplier and it is measured in the Census; it is also a Closing the Gap indicator, so the figures are public, current and contested by nobody.

H2 = travel_time_to_ICU_hospital (hrs) × overcrowded_household_rate × vulnerable_population_share ÷ local_health_workforce_per_1000
Data
  • ABS Data by Region — health, housing, population
  • Census overcrowding (also a Closing the Gap measure)
  • Hospital locations and role delineation — AIHW
  • Remoteness and travel time — ASGS
Why it varies by LGA

Torres Strait island communities and Cape York shires score at the extreme on every input simultaneously. That is not a modelling artefact — it is the actual situation, and it is why the factor is worth computing.

H3Malicious use · manipulation weight high

Synthetic warning during a live emergency

A fabricated evacuation instruction — cloned voice, spoofed official channel — moves people toward the hazard instead of away from it.

Scored factor — warning-channel monopoly

The harm is not the fake itself. It is the absence of a second channel to check it against. Where the council is the only trusted local voice, where there is one mobile tower, where the community speaks a language the official feed does not, a single convincing fake has no competition.

Weight this by how often the LGA is actually in an emergency — disaster recovery funding activations give you that directly, per council, as a count.

H3 = DRFA_activations_5yr (count) × channel_monopoly (1 / distinct_warning_channels) × (1 + low_english_proficiency_share) × (1 - digital_inclusion_score/100)
Data
  • Disaster Recovery Funding Arrangements activations by LGA — Queensland Reconstruction Authority
  • Australian Digital Inclusion Index — access, affordability, ability
  • Language and English proficiency — ABS
  • Mobile and NBN coverage
Why it varies by LGA

Brisbane has radio, television, three newspapers, an SES presence and a council comms team. A Cape York community in cyclone season may have one Facebook page and a satellite link.

H4Malfunction weight medium-high

Automated decision failure with no alternative provider

Not one person wrongly refused, but a whole community's only route to a service failing silently and simultaneously.

Scored factor — service substitutability

In Brisbane a failed council system is an inconvenience; you drive to a service centre, you call, you email, you go to a state office instead. In a remote community the council may be the only government presence in town, and Indigenous councils deliver in-community what the State provides elsewhere — so the function set that can fail is wider and the fallback set is narrower.

H4 = (1 / alternative_access_points) × remoteness_weight × council_function_breadth × (1 - digital_inclusion_score/100)
Data
  • Remoteness — ASGS
  • Indigenous council flag and delivered service set
  • Service centre and agency locations
  • ADII
Watch out

“Council function breadth” is the one input here with no ready dataset. You will have to code it from council websites — keep the coding rule written down.

H5Malfunction · institutional weight medium-high

Loss of institutional function

The council itself is incapacitated — by intrusion, or by dependence on a single vendor that fails — and there is no internal capability to run the service manually.

Scored factor — institutional thinness

Two things determine whether a council can survive its systems going down: how many people it has indoors, and how much of its money it controls. Grant-funded money is committed money; own-source revenue is the only discretionary capacity a council really has, and Indigenous councils have almost none because DOGIT land is largely not valued and therefore not rateable.

This is the factor that may invert. A shire that still runs half its processes on paper has a smaller attack surface and a real manual fallback; a metropolitan council with two hundred integrated systems and no non-digital path has neither. Digitisation is a strength in the natural-hazard index and a liability here. Build it with the three terms separated or you will bury the most interesting result in the framework.

exposure = system_count × digitisation_depth absorptive = manual_fallback_viability # inverse of digitisation consequence = 1 / (indoor_FTE_per_1000 × own_source_revenue_share) H5 = exposure × (1 - absorptive) × consequence × vendor_concentration
Data
  • Personnel, indoor and outdoor FTE — QLD comparative information report
  • Financial inputs and rate revenue — same source
  • Indigenous Councils Funding Program allocations
  • Vendor concentration — council contracts registers
Why this one matters most

It turns a single vendor failure into a correlated one. If forty councils run the same product and none can operate without it, that is one failure with forty simultaneous victims and no observer. Vendor concentration also has no natural-hazard analogue at all, which makes it your cleanest source of divergence from ADRI.

H6Systemic weight low — deliberately

Concentrated labour market shock

Rapid employment loss concentrated in an LGA with a single dominant industry and a rates base that collapses with it.

Scored factor — occupational concentration in exposed roles

Include it, weight it low, and say why in the write-up. The Productivity Commission's August 2025 interim report found large-scale displacement is not currently occurring in Australia, and that the most significant employment effects are not expected for at least a decade. Down-weighting your own factor on the strength of a source that cuts against you is the single cheapest rigor signal available to you.

H6 = employment_share_in_exposed_ANZSCO × industry_concentration (HHI) × (1 - own_source_revenue_diversity)
Data
  • Census occupation and industry by LGA — ABS
  • Published AI exposure indices — run at least two and report where they disagree
Honest note

Exposure indices are contested and the ANZSCO-to-index crosswalk is lossy. This factor is the weakest in the register and should be presented that way.

Mapping ADRI's capacity themes to AI

Read this before you publish a number

What this index cannot do

You cannot validate it, and you must not imply that you have. ADRI's indicators are grounded in hazards that recur — there are decades of floods and fires to check them against. The catastrophic AI outcomes in this register have not occurred. That means every weight in every formula above is a judgement, not a fitted parameter, and no amount of arithmetic makes it otherwise. An index that looks precise about an unprecedented event is the most dangerous artefact you could hand a council.

So do these four things.

  • Do not publish a single composite score. Publish the six factors separately. A council should be able to see which factor drives its own exposure and go fix that one thing. A rolled-up number hides exactly the information that makes it useful.
  • Separate the measured from the assumed, visibly. Travel time to an ICU, overcrowding, remoteness, connectivity and own-source revenue are measured. The hazard scenarios are assumed. Put them in different columns and use different styling so nobody has to take your word for which is which.
  • Ship the weights as a control, not a constant. If the reader can move a slider and watch the ranking change, you have demonstrated the sensitivity honestly instead of asserting a result. This is also the best possible demo moment.
  • State the falsifier. Write down, before you run it, what result would tell you the framework is wrong — for instance, if exposure turns out uncorrelated with remoteness across every factor, your theory of the case has failed.
The claim that survives all of this. Australia has a government-published, peer-reviewed index of which communities absorb shocks badly. It was built for natural hazards. Every catastrophic AI scenario in the international literature lands through the same channels — essential services, health access, warning systems, institutional capacity. The communities already known to be least resilient are therefore the ones with the most to lose from an entirely new hazard class, and no one has checked. That is a defensible, novel, two-day question.

Caveats

The formulas here are proposed starting points, not established methods — they are written to be argued with, and every weight in them is a judgement. Thresholds and definitions (the SOCI 100,000-connection figure in particular) were confirmed as at August 2026 but should be checked against the primary instrument before they appear in a submission. The Australian Disaster Resilience Index theme structure is summarised from published descriptions; read Volume II before mirroring its computation. Several indicators named here are available at SA2 rather than LGA and will need concordance, which introduces error you should quantify rather than absorb silently.
